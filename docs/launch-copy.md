## Page: Our Story (`/pages/our-story`, template *page.our-story*)

**Title:** Our Story

We're a couple in Colorado Springs. One of us knits; the other keeps the
coffee going and the books straight. Summit & Stitch started at our
kitchen table, the way most good things around here do — slowly, by hand,
and a little stubbornly.

Every piece we sell was knit by hand, one skein at a time. We don't
manufacture. We don't chase seasons. We make a small number of things we
believe in, and we make them so they last: good merino and alpaca, honest
construction, colors pulled from the places we live — linen mornings,
navy nights, slate rock, brick-red canyon walls.

We believe things worth having are worth waiting for, and worth keeping.
That belief runs deeper than knitwear for us — it's how we try to live,
how we work, and why we'd rather make ten pieces that last a decade than
a hundred that don't.

If you buy something from us, it was made for you after you ordered it.
It ships when it's ready — usually two to three weeks — with our thanks
and a little wear-it-well from both of us.

You'll find us most weekends at the farmers market, same table, same
thermos. Come say hi.

— Summit & Stitch, Colorado Springs

## Page: Care (`/pages/care`, template *page.care*)

**Title:** Caring for Your Knits

A hand-knit piece will outlast almost anything in your closet if you
treat it gently. Here's all it asks:

**Washing**

- Hand wash in cool water with a wool-safe soap (no detergent, no
  softener).
- Don't wring. Press the water out between two towels.
- Lay flat to dry, reshaped, away from direct sun and heat.

**Between washes**

- Wool cleans itself better than you'd think. Air your piece overnight
  instead of washing — most of the time that's all it needs.
- Fold, don't hang. Hanging stretches the shoulders and cuffs.

**Pilling**

- A little pilling is natural for soft yarns and shows up where fabric
  rubs. Remove pills gently with a sweater comb. The fabric settles down
  after the first few wears.

**Storage**

- Store clean, folded, and dry. For the off-season, a breathable cotton
  bag and a cedar block will keep everything as it should be.

If something happens to your piece — a snag, a hole, a moth with good
taste — write us at hello@summitandstitch.co before you retire it. Most
things can be mended, and we'd rather repair than replace.
